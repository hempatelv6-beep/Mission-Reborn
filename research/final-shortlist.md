# Final Shortlist (R2): draft for review

*13 Nov 2026. Labels: ✔ = checked against an official page this session (see `sources.md`). [K] = known fact, **to be checked in R3** before it appears on the site.*

## How we chose
We scored each machine on six things: historical importance, interesting equipment, a good "what happened" story, official photos, a published location, and how different it is from the others already on the list. Being famous was not enough to get a machine onto the list.

## Moon (4)
| # | Machine | Why include it | Ending / status | Location source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Apollo 12 LM "Intrepid" descent stage** | A crewed landing deliberately aimed close to an older robot lander, which proved pinpoint landing was possible [K] | Descent stage still on the surface; ascent stage **deliberately crashed** into the Moon ✔ | NSSDCA: 3.0128°S, 23.4219°W ✔ |
| 2 | **Apollo 12 ALSEP** | A science station with **6 instruments** ✔ (seismometer, magnetometer, solar wind, ion and dust detectors) powered by a nuclear SNAP-27 generator ✔. It shows what humans *left running* | **Switched off** when ALSEP operations ended in 1977 [K] | NSSDCA: 3.0094°S, 23.4246°W ✔ |
| 3 | **Surveyor 3** | A robot lander that tested whether the soil could hold up an LM ✔. It bounced twice on landing ✔. Apollo 12 crew **walked to it and brought its camera and scoop home** ✔ | Last contact 4 May 1967 ✔. **Inactive**, with parts removed | science.nasa.gov gives "about 3°S, 23.41°W" ✔. ? Look for a more precise LRO value |
| 4 | **Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV-1, Apollo 15)** | The first car on the Moon [K]. Wire-mesh wheels, battery power, navigation and TV relay make for great equipment to show | **Parked and left behind** [K] | ? Not in the NSSDCA table. Find the parking-spot coordinates from LROC in R3, or use the Apollo 15 LM site and label it "near" |

**Why these four together:** machines 1–3 sit within about 200 m of each other ✔ (the Surveyor 3 page gives ~180 m). That makes one *connected story*: a robot proves the ground is safe, and humans come back to visit it. The LRV adds Apollo 15's different site and kind of exploring.
**Alternatives considered:** Apollo 11 "Eagle" (more famous, but its experiment package was simpler, so it adds less variety). The Apollo 11, 14 and 15 laser retroreflectors could become a fact inside the Apollo story instead of a separate machine.

## Mars (6)
| # | Machine | Why include it | Ending / status [all K, to verify in R3] |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | **Viking 1 lander** | The first long-lived US lander, with life-detection experiments | **Lost contact** in 1982 after a bad command |
| 6 | **Sojourner** | The first wheeled rover on Mars, small and simple | Contact with the Pathfinder base lost in 1997 |
| 7 | **Spirit** | Found evidence of past water; its broken wheel dug up silica | **Stuck in sand**, then fell silent in 2010 |
| 8 | **Opportunity** | Planned for 90 days, it worked for about 15 years | Dust storm in 2018 covered its solar panels |
| 9 | **InSight** | A seismometer that "listened" to marsquakes, plus a heat-flow probe ("mole") that couldn't dig in | Dust on its solar panels; mission ended in 2022 |
| 10 | **Ingenuity** | The first powered flight on another world: 72 flights ✔ | Last flight 18 Jan 2024; mission completed 25 Jan 2024 ✔. ? What it is doing now still needs checking |

**Optional 11: Phoenix.** It touched water ice near the north pole, which adds a polar location and a "frozen in the winter" ending. Add it only if time allows.
**Left out of the MVP:** Viking 2 (too similar to Viking 1), and Curiosity and Perseverance (still working, so they don't fit our theme of left-behind machines).

## Variety check
- **Endings:** deliberately crashed, switched off, parts taken home, parked, lost contact, stuck, dust on solar panels, retired. Every story ends differently.
- **Machine types:** lander, science station, rover, helicopter.
- **Equipment highlights:** seismometers (ALSEP, InSight), wheels (LRV, Sojourner, Spirit), solar panels, a nuclear generator, rotors, a robot arm or scoop.
